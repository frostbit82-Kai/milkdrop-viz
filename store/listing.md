# Store listing — BomsAI Visualizer

Source of truth for every storefront. Written once, pasted into the Microsoft
Store dashboard, the site's product page, and anywhere else. Keep it in sync
rather than rewriting per store.

**The positioning, in one line:** projectM's own player is free, well reviewed
and plays the same presets. It does not help you find the good ones, keep a
clip of them, or put them on your desktop. That is what this sells.

---

## Name

Store name: **BomsAI Visualizer**
Subtitle: *A music visualizer for MilkDrop presets*

Settled. The app was called MilkDrop Visualizer until 2026-09-07; "MilkDrop" is
Ryan Geiss's name for his own software, and using someone else's software name
*as your product name* in a paid listing is what gets a listing pulled after it
is already selling. MilkDrop now appears only where it belongs — as a
description of what the app plays, which is normal and which every competitor
does, projectM's own Steam listing included.

## Description  (10,000 char max)

> Everyone who installs a MilkDrop preset pack has the same evening. Ten
> thousand presets, an hour of watching, four or five that are genuinely
> beautiful — and no way to ever find those five again.
>
> BomsAI Visualizer is built around that problem. It plays MilkDrop presets,
> reacting to whatever is coming out of your speakers, and it gives you the one
> thing a ten-thousand-preset folder needs: a way to keep score.
>
> **Rate as you watch.** Every preset takes a rating from 0 to 10, set with a
> keypress while it is on screen. No stopping, no dialog, no tagging session.
>
> **Then the library sorts itself.** Sort by rating and your best presets are
> at the top. Turn on rated shuffle and only the ones you liked come up.
> Delete every 0-rated preset in one action and the folder is permanently
> smaller and permanently better. Do that for a week and ten thousand presets
> become your two hundred.
>
> **Name what a set is for.** A rating says how good a preset is. A collection
> says what it is *for* — "chill", "party", "the ones that look good behind the
> icons". Add whatever is on screen to one from the menu, then play only that
> collection.
>
> **The browser shows where you are.** A live list of the whole playlist with
> the current preset highlighted and its stars beside it. Jump to any of them.
>
> **Search ten thousand presets by name.** Type part of a name and the list
> narrows as you go. Everything else — sorting, rating, shuffle — keeps working
> on what is left.
>
> **The library grows its own thumbnails.** Watch a preset for a few seconds
> and its picture is kept, taken from the frame that was actually on screen,
> and shown beside its name from then on. There is no two-hour batch render
> over your whole folder; the pictures that appear are of the presets you sat
> through, which are the ones you were going to look for again.
>
> **Keep a clip.** One key starts recording what is on screen; another stops
> it. MP4 for a video — with the sound that was playing over it — or a GIF for
> something to post. The presets that made you stop and stare are now something
> you can send to somebody.
>
> **Put it on your desktop.** Wallpaper mode draws the visuals on the Windows
> desktop, behind your icons, reacting to whatever you are playing. A tray icon
> keeps the menu and your ratings a click away.
>
> **It knows when to stop.** Nothing is playing, or a game is full-screen in
> front of it? The visuals pause and give the machine back. Sound returns and
> so do they. A wallpaper you never have to think about is the only kind worth
> having.
>
> **Or leave it as your screen saver.** The installer will set it up, and any
> key or a real mouse movement brings you back.
>
> **Every monitor is a choice.** With more than one screen connected, pick
> which one fullscreen and wallpaper mode use.
>
> **It reacts to everything.** Audio is captured from your system output, so it
> visualises Spotify, YouTube, a game, a DAW, a Zoom call — anything that makes
> sound. Nothing to route, no virtual cable to install, no "which input" to get
> wrong. Switch outputs mid-song and it follows.
>
> **Textures work.** MilkDrop 2 presets that reference texture files are the
> best-looking ones there are, and they are exactly the ones that fall back to
> a plain waveform in players that cannot find the textures. Point this at your
> texture folder and they render as their authors intended.
>
> **Presets install as they come.** Packs are nested folders, not flat ones.
> This loads them recursively, so a pack works the moment it lands — no
> flattening ten thousand files into one directory first.
>
> **It will fetch a pack for you.** Get presets downloads a curated pack from
> the public repository it comes from, unpacks it, and points the app at it.
> One button on a fresh install.
>
> **Yours, offline, permanently.** No account. No sign-in. No licence server,
> no update check, no telemetry, no crash reporting. It has never sent anything
> anywhere and it never will. Your ratings and settings are files on your own
> disk. Buy it once and it keeps working with the internet unplugged.
>
> **Change the one you nearly like.** Every preset opens in a panel of ten
> sliders — zoom, rotation, warp, drift, trails, brightness, echo, wave size.
> Move one and the visual changes while you watch it. Save the result under a
> new name and it joins the library as its own preset. The sliders a preset
> animates itself are greyed out, so you are never fighting its own code.
>
> **Swap its pictures.** A preset that draws with a photograph can draw with
> yours instead. Pick the texture, pick your file, and it is that preset now.
> The original pack is never touched.
>
> **Lay your own picture over it.** A logo, a photo, anything: it sits over the
> visuals and stays there while the presets change underneath. Still, spinning,
> drifting, or pulsing to the beat, with size and opacity you set. Up to
> sixteen at once, and they are still there next time you open the app.
>
> **Your ratings are the part you cannot download again.** Everything else here
> can be reinstalled; hours of deciding what you like cannot. Back up ratings
> and collections to a zip in one action, and restore them on another machine.
>
> **Made to be lived with.** Rebindable keys for everything. Dark and light
> themes, or follow Windows. Borderless mode. Preset locking when one is too
> good to let rotate away. Adjustable beat sensitivity, mesh quality and frame
> rate for the machine you actually have.
>
> **About the presets.** They are not included, deliberately. The presets and
> textures are the work of hundreds of artists who released them under their
> own terms, and bundling other people's art into something sold is not
> something we are willing to do. The app fetches them from where their authors
> put them, and reads them from a folder you choose. You get the same library
> either way — it just arrives with the credit pointing in the right direction.

## System requirements

- Windows 10 version 1809 or later, 64-bit
- A GPU with OpenGL 3.3 support (any card from roughly 2012 onward)
- 4 GB RAM
- ~200 MB installed, plus whatever your preset and texture folders take
- Recording needs ffmpeg — on PATH, or dropped into the application folder
- Presets and textures are downloaded separately — the app will fetch a pack

## Age rating questionnaire — answer honestly

The one that matters: **yes, the app contains flashing or strobing imagery.**
That is what a music visualizer is. There is a photosensitivity warning on
first run and a clause in the EULA. Do not answer "no" here to avoid a rating
tier — a store that finds out later pulls the listing.

Everything else is no: no violence, no sexual content, no profanity, no user
interaction, no user-generated content shared between users, no data collected,
no ads, no in-app purchases, no gambling.

## Pricing — decided

**$5.00, no trial, no subscription.**

Was $4.99 while the app was curation alone, then $7.99 once recording and
wallpaper mode landed. Dropped to a flat $5 on 2026-09-08, the owner's call:
*"I've seen full games sell for less than the current asking price, figured $5
would be a steal for what the user gets, plus motivate others to try, due to
affordability."*

The reasoning behind $7.99 was never wrong — nothing free does curation *and*
recording *and* wallpaper, and the ceiling really is around $9.99, where people
compare this to Wallpaper Engine's entire workshop and it loses. What changed
is which end of the range to aim at. This is a first paid app from an unknown
name with no reviews to lean on, and at $5 the decision to try it stops being a
decision. Volume and word of mouth are worth more here than $3 a copy.

The floor is still set by projectM's own free player, which plays the same
presets — so the copy stays about what that player cannot do.

Do not discount at launch. A launch discount off an unknown price is not a
discount, and it burns the one sale event you get.

## ffmpeg — a decision still to make

Recording shells out to ffmpeg. Right now the app looks for it beside the
executable first and on PATH second, and says so in the menu when there is
none. Asking a paying customer to install ffmpeg is the weakest thing in the
product.

Bundling it is the fix, and the licence is the only real question. **Ship an
LGPL build, never a GPL one.** The GPL builds are the ones that carry libx264,
and shipping GPL code inside a paid product is an argument nobody needs to
have. An LGPL Windows build has `h264_mf` — H.264 through Media Foundation,
hardware accelerated — which is as good as libx264 for this. The app already
asks ffmpeg what encoders it has and picks libx264, then h264_mf, then mpeg4,
so an LGPL build works with no code change.

Roughly 40–80 MB depending on the build. That is the whole cost.

## Naming — settled 2026-09-07

Renamed from MilkDrop Visualizer to **BomsAI Visualizer**, with MilkDrop kept as
the descriptor. The Inno Setup `AppId` GUID was deliberately NOT changed: it is
what lets a future build upgrade an install rather than sitting beside it, and
nothing had shipped, so there was no installed base to strand.

What moved with the name, in case something looks wrong later: the Windows
install folder and Start-menu entry, the frozen build's per-user config
directory (`BomsAIVisualizer`), the preset-pack install directory, the download
User-Agent, the Linux command (`bomsai-viz`), the desktop entry and icon
(`bomsai-visualizer.*`), and both installer artifact names.
